  <p class="eyebrow">Yahya Rabbani / Research Vision</p>
  <h1>Research Vision</h1>
  <p class="keywords">Physics-informed design<span>|</span>Autonomous experimentation<span>|</span>Adaptive materials &amp; sensing</p>
</section>

<p class="statement">My goal is to build an <strong>experimentally grounded framework for discovering functional materials and optical sensors</strong>: use <strong>physics</strong> to inform models, <strong>experiments</strong> to test predictions, and <strong>AI</strong> to choose the most promising next steps. Optical nanosensors are the starting platform; autonomous materials discovery is the destination.</p>

  <figure class="thrust-media">

  <div>
    <span class="tag">Thrust 1</span>
    <h2>Physics-informed materials design</h2>
    <p>I will integrate <strong>physics-based modeling with machine learning</strong> to connect material interfaces and functional properties. Beginning with <strong>ssDNA–SWCNT systems</strong>, I aim to understand how sequence shapes wrapping, interaction, and optical behavior, and to use that understanding to guide functionalization strategies and the rational design of nanoscale sensor architectures.</p>
    <p>Experimental validation will connect predictions with measured <strong>selectivity, response, and stability</strong>, so that models are trusted only where they are confirmed.</p>
  </div>
</div>

    <span class="tag">Thrust 2</span>
    <h2>Autonomous experimentation</h2>
    <p>I will build on <strong>high-throughput nanosensor preparation</strong> and <strong>near-infrared characterization</strong> by integrating robotic liquid handling, automated measurements, and <strong>AI-guided experiment selection</strong>.</p>
    <p>The goal is a <strong>closed-loop workflow</strong> that learns from each round and prioritizes the next set of experiments, accelerating discovery cycles by coupling data-driven decisions with physically interpretable models, and extending from sensors to broader materials discovery.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="thrust">
  <figure class="thrust-media">

    <span class="tag">Thrust 3</span>
    <h2>Adaptive materials and sensing</h2>
    <p>I will extend <strong>programmable material interfaces</strong> to new targets and develop <strong>sensor arrays</strong> that distinguish complex samples through combined optical response patterns. Diverse sensor libraries and machine learning will map the resulting <strong>optical fingerprints</strong> to specific analytes, enabling more robust sensing in biological, food, and environmental settings.</p>
    <p>Biomedical directions include exploring disease-associated molecular patterns with independently characterized samples and clinical collaborators. These are proposed directions; establishing them will require careful validation.</p>
