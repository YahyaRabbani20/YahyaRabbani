  <h1>Research Vision</h1>
  <p class="keywords">Physics-informed design<span>|</span>Autonomous experimentation<span>|</span>Adaptive materials &amp; sensing</p>

My goal is to build an experimentally grounded framework for discovering functional materials and optical sensors: use physics to inform models, experiments to test predictions, and AI to choose promising next steps.

## Physics-informed materials design
I will integrate physics-based modeling with machine learning to connect material interfaces and functional properties. Beginning with ssDNA–SWCNT systems, I aim to guide functionalization strategies and understand sequence-dependent optical behavior. Experimental validation will connect predictions with measured selectivity, response, and stability.

## Autonomous experimentation
I will build on high-throughput nanosensor preparation and near-infrared characterization by integrating robotic liquid handling, automated measurements, and AI-guided experiment selection. The goal is a closed-loop workflow that learns from each round and prioritizes the next set of experiments, extending from sensors to broader materials discovery.

## Adaptive materials and sensing
I will extend programmable material interfaces to new targets and develop arrays that distinguish complex samples through combined optical response patterns. Diverse sensor libraries and machine learning will support molecular fingerprinting across biological, food, and environmental applications.

Biomedical directions include exploring disease-associated molecular patterns with independently characterized samples and clinical collaborators. These are proposed research directions; establishing diagnostic performance will require dedicated validation.
