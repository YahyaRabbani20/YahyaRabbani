<section class="page-banner">
  <h1>Research Vision</h1>
  <p class="keywords">Physics-informed design<span>|</span>Autonomous experimentation<span>|</span>Adaptive materials &amp; sensing</p>
</section>

<p class="statement">My goal is to build a way of <strong>discovering functional materials and optical sensors</strong> that stays grounded in experiments. <strong>Physics</strong> helps build the models, <strong>experiments</strong> test their predictions, and <strong>AI</strong> chooses the most promising next steps. Optical nanosensors are where I start, and autonomous materials discovery is where I want this work to go.</p>

    <span class="tag">Thrust 1</span>
    <h2>Physics-informed materials design</h2>
    <p>I want to combine <strong>physics-based modeling with machine learning</strong> to connect what happens at a material's surface with how the material performs. Starting with <strong>ssDNA-SWCNT systems</strong>, I aim to understand how the DNA sequence affects wrapping, molecular interactions, and optical behavior, and then use that knowledge to design better sensors.</p>
    <p>Every prediction will be checked in the lab against measured <strong>selectivity, response, and stability</strong>, so the models are only trusted where experiments confirm them.</p>
  </div>
</div>

    <span class="tag">Thrust 2</span>
    <h2>Autonomous experimentation</h2>
    <p>I plan to build on my <strong>high-throughput sensor preparation</strong> and <strong>near-infrared measurements</strong> by adding robotic liquid handling, automated measurements, and <strong>AI that chooses the next experiments</strong>.</p>
    <p>The goal is a <strong>closed loop</strong> that learns from each round and decides what to test next. Combining data-driven decisions with physically meaningful models should make discovery much faster, first for sensors and later for other materials.</p>
  </div>
</div>

  <figure class="thrust-media">

    <span class="tag">Thrust 3</span>
    <h2>Adaptive materials and sensing</h2>
    <p>I want to extend <strong>programmable material surfaces</strong> to new targets and build <strong>sensor arrays</strong> that tell complex samples apart by their combined optical response. With diverse sensor libraries and machine learning, these <strong>optical fingerprints</strong> can be linked to specific molecules, which makes sensing more reliable in biological, food, and environmental samples.</p>
    <p>In medicine, I would like to study disease-related molecular patterns together with clinical collaborators and well-characterized samples. These are future directions and will need careful validation.</p>
